Privacy Policy

“Mom, I’m Right Here” · updated August 27, 2026

“Mom, I’m Right Here” is a way to know, every day, that someone you love is fine: a parent answers with one button in Telegram, and the family sees the answer in the app. The service is built around one simple principle: we store only what the family has chosen to show each other — and we don’t look at it ourselves.

What we never do

  • We don’t track location — there is no geolocation in the app or the bot.
  • We don’t read chats — the bot only sees messages written directly to it.
  • We don’t sell or share data with third parties, and we show no ads.
  • We don’t use the family’s data to train anything.
  • We never ask for SMS codes, passwords, or money in a conversation.

What we store

  • The parent’s name, city and time zone, and their preferred morning time — so we write on time and by name.
  • The parent’s Telegram account identifier — so the bot knows whom to write to.
  • Button answers (“all is well” / “not great”) and their time — this is the history the family sees.
  • Messages, voice notes and photos the parent chose to send the bot for the family, and postcards the family sent to the parent.
  • Medication names and times, if the family enabled reminders.
  • Answers to the “question of the week” — family stories visible only to the family.
  • The child’s display name in the app and a technical token for phone notifications.
Everything listed above is visible only to your family: the parent and the people they or their children connected via the family link. There is no “public” access of any kind.

Where it lives

Data is stored in a cloud database (Supabase) and processed by Cloudflare infrastructure. Connections are encrypted. Postcard photos are kept only until delivery and deleted right after it; if delivery never happens, the photo deletes itself within a week.

How to delete everything

  • The parent can message the bot /stop at any moment and choose “Stop completely” — the bot goes silent and their data is deleted.
  • The family can ask us to delete everything — the account, the history, the weekly stories — by writing to us from any connected device.
